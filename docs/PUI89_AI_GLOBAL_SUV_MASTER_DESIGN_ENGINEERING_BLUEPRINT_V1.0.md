# PUI89 AI Global SUV — Master Design & Engineering Blueprint

**Document:** PUI89-SUV-MASTER-BLUEPRINT  
**Version:** 1.0 (foundational proposal)  
**Date:** 2026-11-20  
**Status:** Proposed design and engineering baseline — not a production specification, implemented vehicle, validated system, homologation package or certification.  
**Product:** PUI89 AI Global SUV  
**Design intent:** Global-grade capability, original PUI89 architecture, human-centred mobility.

> **Benchmark policy:** Volvo is the safety benchmark; Mercedes-Benz is the luxury and cabin-experience benchmark; BMW is the engineering and vehicle-integration benchmark; Tesla and Rivian are software-defined vehicle (SDV) benchmarks. These are learning references only. PUI89 must develop its own architecture, design language, requirements, interfaces and verification evidence. No proprietary design, software, confidential information or protected intellectual property is to be copied.

## 1. Executive design direction

PUI89 AI Global SUV is an original global-market SUV concept with a software-first, modular and upgrade-conscious vehicle-intelligence architecture. It is intended to combine:
- safety-led vehicle and occupant protection;
- premium, calm and ergonomic user experience;
- disciplined whole-vehicle engineering;
- an updatable, modular and secure software architecture;
- privacy-first, local-capable multimodal intelligence;
- practical serviceability, repairability and lifecycle ownership.

**PUI89 design principle:** “Build what you wish existed as a user.”  
**Engineering philosophy:** “Easy to Build. Easy to Repair. Built to Grow.”

The vehicle form, segment, dimensions, seating, cargo volume, mass, platform, suspension geometry, powertrain and target markets remain open engineering decisions. Earlier concept artwork is illustrative only and does not approve those specifications.

## 3. PUI89 design identity

### 3.1 Visual language
- Core palette: Deep Navy, Electric Cyan and Titanium Silver.
- Distinctive PUI89 lighting signature, front and rear, to be developed as original design work.
- Confident SUV stance, purposeful proportions, clean surfaces and functional aerodynamic treatment.
- Robust, replaceable exterior protection elements where engineering and styling permit.
- Wheel, tyre, ground-clearance and approach/departure decisions remain dependent on the chosen vehicle class and validated use cases.
- Avoid decorative sensors, false vents, non-functional displays and styling that obstructs visibility or service access.

### 3.2 Interior experience
- Calm, uncluttered, premium and ergonomic cabin.
- Driver-first instrument and alert hierarchy; critical warnings must not be hidden in menus.
- Clear physical access to safety-critical controls where required by human-factors and regulatory review.
- Multilingual interface architecture; Thai and English are initial language targets, subject to translation and usability validation.
- Accessibility, child-seat interfaces, occupant protection, visibility, ingress/egress and storage are requirements to study, not claims of completed design.
- Materials must be evaluated for durability, cleanability, heat, UV exposure, repairability and regional availability.

## 4. Exterior design requirements

1. Establish a unique PUI89 SUV proportion and body identity through original sketches, package studies and user research.
2. Define visibility envelopes, lighting performance, pedestrian protection, crash structures, bumper geometry and repair zones with qualified automotive engineers.
3. Integrate cameras, radar, ultrasonic or other sensors only after coverage, occlusion, cleaning, calibration, environmental and service studies.
4. Avoid sensor placements that create avoidable blind zones or depend on unvalidated transparent covers.
5. Validate water management, drainage, sealing, corrosion resistance, dust ingress and thermal exposure for each target market.
6. Assess aerodynamic drag, wind noise, cooling airflow and energy impact through simulation and later controlled testing.
7. Keep exterior dimensions, doors, seating configuration, wheelbase, track, ground clearance and luggage capacity TBD pending package and platform studies.

**Evidence gate:** package drawings, visibility study, lighting requirements, pedestrian-protection review, sensor field-of-view study, serviceability review and market-specific regulatory assessment.

## 5. Interior, HMI and occupant experience

### 5.1 Cabin architecture
- Driver cockpit, passenger zones, seating, restraints, airbags, child-seat anchors, HVAC, storage and ingress/egress must be designed as one integrated package.
- Seat count and cabin dimensions remain TBD.
- Comfort targets should be measurable: ergonomic reach, posture, visibility, noise/vibration/harshness, thermal comfort and accessibility.
- Cabin materials and finishes require supplier provenance, durability, repair and chemical-compliance evidence.

### 5.2 Human-machine interface
- Separate driving-critical information from convenience and entertainment functions.
- Prioritise alerts by severity, urgency, confidence and required driver action.
- Use concise wording, consistent symbols, understandable audio/haptic cues and a documented alert-escalation policy.
- Prevent avoidable distraction, alert fatigue and ambiguous automation status.
- Local core safety information must remain available without internet connectivity.
- Voice features, if adopted, must be permissioned, transparent, locally processed by default where feasible, and never treated as the only way to access essential controls.

### 5.3 Privacy
- Data minimisation and local processing/storage by default.
- No upload of cabin audio, faces, video, location history, home/work routes or vehicle telemetry to unapproved services.
- Explicit purpose, consent, retention, deletion, access and export rules for each data class.
- Connected services must degrade safely when offline or when consent is withdrawn.

## 6. Chassis, body and vehicle dynamics

The chassis and body are safety-critical physical systems and require qualified automotive engineering. This blueprint defines study areas, not final designs.

- Body-in-white, crash load paths, occupant survival space and restraint integration.
- Suspension geometry, steering system, tyres, wheels, brakes and stability systems.
- Structural stiffness, fatigue, corrosion, underbody protection and repairability.
- Ride comfort, handling, braking, steering feel, rollover resistance and load sensitivity.
- Thermal management, cooling airflow, acoustic isolation and vibration paths.
- Mass distribution, centre of gravity, axle loads and payload must be established from a complete vehicle package.
- Any off-road, towing, water-crossing or high-load capability requires a separately defined use case and validated engineering evidence.

**No chassis geometry, crash rating, braking performance, water-wading depth, towing rating or vehicle dynamics result is currently claimed.**

## 7. Powertrain and energy architecture

PUI89 will evaluate powertrain options using transparent, market-specific evidence rather than committing prematurely.

### 7.1 Options to compare
- ICE benchmark.
- Full hybrid (HEV) study.
- Plug-in hybrid (PHEV) study.
- Battery electric vehicle (BEV) study.

The previous HEV-first direction remains a research priority, not a final product decision. The AI architecture must remain powertrain-neutral.

### 7.2 Comparison criteria
- Real-world energy/fuel consumption under comparable cycles and conditions.
- Emissions and applicable regional regulations.
- Thermal management, battery/fuel safety and crash response.
- Durability, serviceability, repair ecosystem and parts availability.
- Total cost of ownership, lifecycle impacts and infrastructure fit.
- Electrical architecture, low-voltage supply and compute power budget.

No fuel-economy, range, acceleration, emissions, battery-life, cost or reliability figure may be published without traceable comparable evidence. PUI89 AI must not command propulsion, traction battery management, charging or high-voltage isolation.

## 8. Sensors and perception architecture

Sensor choice follows defined use cases and safety analysis; it must not begin with a shopping list or an assumption that more sensors always means safer performance.

### 8.1 Candidate sensor groups
1. **Vision:** forward/rear/side cameras and cabin-facing sensors only where justified, permissioned and validated.
2. **Radar:** candidate short/medium/long-range sensing for supported object and range cues.
3. **Ultrasonic/proximity:** close-range manoeuvring support where validated.
4. **Inertial and motion:** IMU and supported wheel/vehicle motion signals through approved interfaces.
5. **Position and map context:** GNSS and legally usable map/road context with freshness and integrity checks.
6. **Environmental:** rain, ambient temperature, visibility and other justified environmental measurements.
7. **Vehicle health:** read-only, documented signals for battery/thermal/electrical/tyre/diagnostic context where a verified interface exists.

LiDAR, depth cameras, thermal cameras or additional modalities are optional research candidates only; no adoption is assumed.

### 8.2 Sensor registry requirements
For every candidate sensor, record:
- manufacturer, model, country of origin, ownership and full supply-chain provenance;
- purpose, modality, installation zone and field of view;
- operating range, accuracy, latency, update rate and environmental limits;
- occlusion, contamination, interference and failure modes;
- calibration method, interval, health monitoring and replacement procedure;
- data format, timestamp, synchronisation, units and quality indicators;
- confidence, uncertainty, provenance and missing/stale/conflicting-data handling;
- cybersecurity, firmware support, licensing and end-of-life status.

No sensor performance is assumed until measured on the selected platform in relevant conditions.

## 9. PUI89 AI Core and heterogeneous compute

### 9.1 Architecture objective
Develop a PUI89-owned vehicle intelligence layer that coordinates software workloads across available CPU, GPU, NPU, MCU, memory, sensors and power budgets. PUI89 is not proposing to manufacture semiconductor chips. The focus is efficient orchestration, model sizing, quantisation, scheduling, thermal/power-aware execution, edge inference and graceful fallback.

### 9.2 Logical layers
1. **Sensor and Vehicle Adapters:** approved, documented, preferably read-only data interfaces.
2. **Input Integrity:** source authentication, provenance, timestamps, units, freshness, calibration and quality checks.
3. **Multimodal Perception:** task-specific vision, radar and other validated modality processing.
4. **World Model:** tracks, scene context, road state, environmental context, uncertainty and explicit unknowns.
5. **Risk and Reasoning:** hazard severity, proximity, movement, context, confidence and scenario-specific risk.
6. **Safety Supervisor:** deterministic rules, authority boundaries, stale-data gates, degraded modes and abstention.
7. **Driver Advisory and HMI:** explainable, prioritised alerts and recommendations.
8. **Evidence and Diagnostics:** traceability, event records, model/version provenance, failures and recovery.
9. **Compute Orchestrator:** resource budgets, workload scheduling, CPU/GPU/NPU/MCU selection, thermal and power constraints, fallback and load shedding.

### 9.3 Shared AI Core contracts
Use canonical PUI89 Shared AI Core contracts rather than creating incompatible parallel schemas:
- Observation
- WorldState
- ActionProposal
- FailureReport
- Evidence Passport / provenance record where supported by the shared standard.

Every proposal must preserve source evidence, timestamp, uncertainty, known limitations and human authority. Vehicle adapter conformance must be tested against pinned canonical schemas.

### 9.4 Heterogeneous compute policy
- Benchmark CPU-only baseline first; add accelerators only when measured benefit justifies complexity.
- Record peak and sustained latency, memory, power, thermal behaviour and workload contention.
- Quantisation/model compression must be evaluated for task-specific accuracy loss and failure modes.
- Schedule safety-relevant perception with bounded latency and explicit resource reservations.
- When overloaded, shed non-critical workloads before safety-relevant tasks; if safe performance cannot be maintained, issue a degraded/unknown state.
- No unsupported hard real-time claim.
- No model, firmware, package or accelerator is approved without origin, licence, security, maintenance and hardware-fit review.

### 9.5 AI authority boundary
Initial scope is software research and driver advisory only. No direct steering, throttle, braking, propulsion, transmission, airbag, restraint, battery-management, charging or high-voltage isolation command path is authorised. Any future change requires a new safety architecture, qualified review, regulatory analysis and explicit project approval.

## 10. Safety architecture — Volvo-inspired, PUI89-owned

Safety must be designed across the whole vehicle and lifecycle, not delegated to AI.

- Hazard identification and risk assessment for vehicle, occupant, vulnerable road users, environment, software, sensors, compute, power and human-machine interaction.
- Independent conventional vehicle safety systems remain authoritative and must not depend on the PUI89 AI stack.
- Fail-silent/fail-safe behaviour must be defined for each interface and failure mode; “safe state” is system-specific and must not be assumed to mean an abrupt vehicle stop.
- Explicit handling of sensor loss, disagreement, occlusion, contamination, stale data, bad calibration, compute overload, overheating, power interruption and network compromise.
- Driver workload, distraction, over-trust, alert fatigue and misuse analysis.
- Emergency guidance must be conservative and evidence-based.
- Unknown flood depth/current/passability means **unknown — do not enter**. No safe wading depth or water-crossing capability is inferred.
- Suspected smoke/fire/thermal event: prioritise occupant evacuation and emergency services; do not instruct occupants to touch high-voltage parts or fight a traction-battery fire.
- No safety rating, crash performance, ADAS level, functional-safety compliance or regulatory approval is claimed.

## 11. Cybersecurity, privacy and data sovereignty

Apply PUI89 Security Core, Repository Defense, Evidence & Recovery and the project's zero-loss policy.

- Threat modelling across vehicle interfaces, sensors, diagnostic ports, update channels, mobile applications, cloud connectivity, supply chain and service tools.
- Secure boot, signed updates, authenticated interfaces, least privilege, key lifecycle, vulnerability response and software bill of materials are requirements for future design review, not claims of implementation.
- Network segmentation and strict separation between safety-critical vehicle systems and advisory AI.
- Default-deny external sharing and remote access; local-first processing and storage.
- Minimise personally identifiable and behavioural data; define purpose limitation, retention and user control.
- Preserve auditability without collecting unnecessary cabin or location data.
- No unapproved remote control, telemetry export, data resale or third-party access.
- Recovery, rollback and safe update interruption handling must be tested before deployment.

## 12. Zero China Supply-Chain and Technology Dependence

This is a mandatory PUI89 project constraint.

### 12.1 Exclusion scope
Do not introduce Chinese-origin or China-dependent:
- vehicle platforms, complete vehicles, critical components or manufacturing;
- sensors, compute modules, semiconductor components, batteries, power electronics or firmware;
- AI models, datasets, software stacks, development tools or cloud/hosting infrastructure;
- build, update, telemetry, mapping or remote-service dependencies;
- suppliers or subcontractors whose relevant origin, ownership, control, production location or dependency cannot be verified.

The policy is about supply-chain and technology dependence, not a blanket exclusion of customers by nationality. Global customer access remains subject to applicable laws and approved market-entry arrangements.

### 12.2 Supplier and technology due diligence
For every proposed supplier, component and software dependency, maintain an evidence record for:
- legal entity, beneficial ownership/control and relevant affiliates;
- design authority, manufacturing site, assembly site and country of origin;
- sub-tier suppliers for critical components and materials where reasonably traceable;
- firmware, model, software and cloud dependencies;
- update, support, vulnerability and end-of-life arrangements;
- data access, remote administration and jurisdiction;
- independent documents and date of verification.

**Unknown provenance = not approved.** A distributor's address or brand headquarters alone is not sufficient evidence of origin or independence. Reassess when ownership, manufacturing, software dependencies or subcontractors change.

## 13. Software-defined vehicle lifecycle

PUI89 SDV principles:
- modular services with versioned, documented interfaces;
- separation of safety-critical vehicle systems from non-critical applications and advisory AI;
- reproducible builds, dependency pinning, signed artefacts and software bill of materials;
- staged updates, compatibility checks, rollback and recovery;
- offline-capable essential functions;
- telemetry minimisation and user-controlled diagnostics;
- long-term support, repair documentation and parts/software availability;
- no forced feature degradation through avoidable cloud dependency;
- clear distinction between OTA capability proposed, implemented, tested and approved.

No OTA, remote diagnostics or vehicle network capability is claimed as implemented.

## 14. Global standards and regulatory workstream

Applicable requirements must be mapped by target market, vehicle category, powertrain and intended function. Qualified legal, homologation, functional-safety, cybersecurity and automotive engineering specialists must confirm applicability and current editions.

Initial standards/regulatory investigation register (not a declaration of compliance):
- **UNECE vehicle regulations:** identify applicable regulations by vehicle category and market, including braking, steering, lighting, occupant protection and relevant driver-assistance provisions.
- **ISO 26262:** road-vehicle functional safety for applicable electrical/electronic systems.
- **ISO 21448 (SOTIF):** safety of intended functionality where applicable.
- **ISO/SAE 21434:** road-vehicle cybersecurity engineering.
- **UNECE R155 / R156:** cybersecurity management and software-update management where applicable to the market/type approval.
- **ISO 24089:** software update engineering.
- **ISO 34502 and related scenario-based safety evaluation work:** investigate applicability to automated-driving test scenarios; do not imply autonomous-driving scope.
- **ISO 9001 / IATF 16949:** quality-management framework and automotive supply-chain expectations as applicable.
- **UN 38.3 and applicable battery/transport rules:** evaluate for battery systems and transport.
- Market-specific crash, emissions, consumer, privacy, radio, accessibility and product-liability requirements.

This list is an investigation starting point, not exhaustive legal advice or proof of compliance. Verify current editions, amendments, national adoption and scope before design freeze.

## 15. Validation and evidence strategy

Use staged gates. A document, simulation, demonstration and road-tested vehicle are not interchangeable evidence.

| Gate | Required evidence | Release boundary |
|---|---|---|
| G0 — Concept | User needs, design principles, assumptions, open decisions | Concept communication only |
| G1 — Requirements | Traceable requirements, operating domain, hazard analysis, interface and privacy requirements | Architecture review |
| G2 — Software baseline | Unit tests, schema conformance, synthetic scenarios, reproducible metrics, security review | Software research demonstration |
| G3 — Bench validation | Approved hardware-in-loop/bench tests, sensor calibration and fault injection | Controlled engineering evaluation |
| G4 — Vehicle integration | Qualified integration plan, interface safety case, installation and service documentation | Restricted, authorised test programme only |
| G5 — Closed-course validation | Instrumented, supervised test results, safety driver, incident process and independent review | Limited test evidence; not public-road approval |
| G6 — Homologation and release | Market approvals, regulatory evidence, manufacturing quality, service and support readiness | Market-specific release only |

Metrics must be defined before tests and reported with dataset/source, sample size, scenario coverage, confidence intervals where appropriate, failure cases, hardware/software versions and limitations. Never fabricate performance values or treat synthetic results as real-world validation.

## 16. Manufacturing, serviceability and lifecycle

- Design for assembly, inspection, repair, parts replacement and safe end-of-life handling.
- Prefer modular replaceable units over sealed assemblies where safety, durability and lifecycle economics permit.
- Publish service access, diagnostics, calibration and repair procedures to authorised service channels.
- Evaluate supplier resilience, spare-parts availability, material traceability and regional support.
- Establish warranty, recall, incident reporting and software-support processes before commercial release.
- Assess lifecycle environmental impacts and responsible battery/material sourcing.

No factory, manufacturing partner, production capability, supplier agreement or commercial release is claimed.

## 18. Immediate implementation roadmap

1. **Requirements baseline:** create traceable user, market, packaging, safety, privacy and service requirements.
2. **Original design language:** develop front/rear identity, side profile, cabin layout and colour/material direction without freezing unverified dimensions.
3. **PUI89 vehicle architecture:** define system boundaries, power/compute budgets, sensor registry and read-only interface contracts.
4. **AI Core alignment:** pin canonical shared contracts and build deterministic synthetic test fixtures.
5. **Safety and cybersecurity:** create hazard log, threat model, safety goals, misuse cases and data-flow inventory.
6. **Supply-chain evidence:** establish supplier/component/software provenance register and exclusion gates.
7. **Verification:** define test plans and measurable acceptance criteria before implementation or hardware selection.

### First practical deliverable
Create a traceable **Vehicle Requirements & Decision Register** linked to this blueprint. Prioritise safety, user needs, global usability, privacy, repairability and verified supply-chain provenance before choosing platform, dimensions, powertrain, sensors or compute hardware.
